# UK Gardening Vocabulary and Techniques

**Read this when:** translating from a US-frame source, checking that a term is the right one for UK content, writing app strings or push notifications where the wrong word would jar.

## British English defaults

Use British English throughout. Default vocabulary (not the American equivalents):

- courgette (not zucchini)
- aubergine (not eggplant)
- sweet pepper (not bell pepper)
- coriander (not cilantro)
- swede (not rutabaga)
- chips (oven, fried — not crisps or french fries)
- garden (not yard)
- lawn / grass (not turf, unless laying turf)
- compost heap / compost bin (not compost pile)
- flowerbed, vegetable bed, raised bed (not garden patch)
- allotment, veg patch, kitchen garden, potager (not "vegetable garden")
- glasshouse, greenhouse, polytunnel, cold frame, cloche (each is distinct)
- fleece (for frost protection), enviromesh, environet (for insect exclusion)

## UK-specific techniques and terms

A good gardening voice uses these without explanation:

- **Chitting** — sprouting seed potatoes before planting, late Jan to Feb.
- **Chelsea Chop** — late-May pruning of certain perennials to delay or shorten flowering.
- **Hardening off** — gradually acclimatising indoor-raised plants to outdoor conditions over 1–2 weeks.
- **Pricking out** — transplanting seedlings from their germination tray into individual modules.
- **Earthing up** — drawing soil up around potato or leek stems.
- **Lazy bed (Irish)** — traditional Irish potato cultivation using raised ridges on undisturbed grass with sod folded over.
- **Stove plant** — an old term for a tender plant needing greenhouse heat; rarely used now.
- **Pinching out** — removing the growing tip to encourage branching (or to defeat blackfly on broad beans).
- **Tying in** — securing climbing or wall-trained growth to its support.
- **Stopping** — pinching out the leader on cordon tomatoes once at ceiling height.
- **Blanching** — excluding light from leek shanks or chicory hearts to whiten and sweeten them.

## Terms to avoid or use carefully

- **"Fall"** — never. Always "autumn".
- **"Yard"** — only as a unit of distance; never the place outside the house.
- **"Garbage / trash"** — "rubbish" or "compost"/"general waste" if the distinction matters.
- **"Truck farming"** — US term for market gardening; don't use.
- **"Cilantro"** — never. "Coriander" (or "fresh coriander" if disambiguation needed because the seed is also called coriander).

## Spelling

- -ise not -ize (organise, fertiliser, naturalise)
- -our not -or (colour, flavour, vigour, behaviour)
- -re not -er (centre, fibre, metre — except in the units where it stays metre)
- aluminium not aluminum
- mould not mold (the soil amendment and the fungus alike)
- grey not gray

## Units

- Metres, centimetres, grams, kilograms — never imperial unless the imperial unit is the conventional one in UK gardening:
  - Foot for hedge height is fine.
  - Pounds for fruit harvest is fine.
  - Pints for hand-watering volume is fine.
- Temperatures: °C only.
