# Companion Planting — Evidence vs Folklore

**Read this when:** writing companion-planting recommendations, drafting the companion section of a species page, advising on guild design, or rejecting a recommendation that doesn't hold up.

The principle: lead with what the evidence says, label the rest. Don't dress folklore in confident language. Most "X helps Y grow" claims that aren't about pest control are unsupported — be honest about that on every Grow Daisy page that mentions them.

## Strong evidence

- **Carrots × onions / leeks / chives (Allium)** reduces carrot fly damage. Mechanism: confusion / repellence. Real but partial — netting is more reliable.
- **Brassicas × strong-scented herbs (mint, rosemary, sage, dill)** reduces cabbage white egg-laying. Real, partial.
- **Beans × sweetcorn × squash (the Three Sisters)** is a real polyculture that works in the right climate — UK summers are usually too cool for it to outperform separate beds.
- **Nasturtiums as aphid trap-crop near beans, brassicas, courgette** — real; they're more attractive to aphids than the main crop.
- **Marigolds (*Tagetes patula*) in tomato beds** — secrete alpha-terthienyl which suppresses some root-knot nematodes. Real but for a problem most UK gardens don't have.
- **Flowering herbs (dill, fennel, parsley flowers, coriander flowers) near brassicas and beans** attracts parasitic wasps and hoverflies which control aphids and caterpillars. Real and strong.

## Weak or no evidence

- **Roses × garlic deterring blackspot** — folklore. Mulch and cultivar choice work.
- **Borage "improves strawberry flavour"** — no evidence.
- **Most "X helps Y grow"** claims that aren't about pest control are unsupported.

## Antagonistic (real)

- **Walnut (Juglans)** inhibits many plants via juglone in the root zone. Tomato, blueberry, apple, rhododendron all suffer near walnut.
- **Fennel inhibits many vegetables** — keep it apart from beans, tomato, dill, coriander.
- **Brassicas × strawberries** is widely repeated but the evidence is thin; treat as "probably fine".

## How to write companion content

When writing the "Companions" section of a species page:

1. List **evidence-based positive companions** with a one-line mechanism ("attracts hoverflies which eat aphids", "confuses carrot fly").
2. List **antagonistic plants** with the same rigour.
3. Optionally include a "Folk pairings (not strongly evidenced)" line if the species has famous claims like roses × garlic.
4. Don't pretend folklore is evidence.
