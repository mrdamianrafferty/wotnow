# UK Pests and Diseases — The Constant Rota

**Read this when:** writing about pest or disease pressure on a UK crop, drafting threat-card content for a species page, helping a user diagnose a problem, or building Grow Daisy's threats data model.

## Slugs and snails

The dominant problem on damp soil and in the west of the UK and Ireland. Worst on hosta, lettuce, brassica seedlings, dahlia, delphinium, courgette seedlings.

Controls: nematodes (*Phasmarhabditis hermaphrodita*) applied to warm moist soil, beer traps, copper rings on pots, hand-picking at dusk, encouraging frogs and beetles, ferric phosphate pellets (organic-approved) for severe infestations.

## Tomato and potato blight (Phytophthora infestans)

Sets in when humidity is above 75% for two consecutive nights with temperatures above 10°C ("Smith Periods"). UK outdoor tomato risk is real from July onwards. Outdoor potato risk is real from mid-summer; main-crops in wet years can be lost.

Defences: choose blight-resistant cultivars (Sarpo for potato; Crimson Crush, Mountain Magic for tomato), space plants well, water at the base never on the foliage, remove infected material immediately, in tomatoes growing under cover the risk is much lower.

## Carrot fly

Drilling carrots through a 60-cm-tall fleece barrier or under enviromesh stops them. Or grow in raised beds higher than the fly flies. Cultivars like 'Resistafly' help but aren't immune.

## Cabbage white butterfly

Netting against caterpillars is the only reliable defence. Encouraging parasitic wasps helps. Hand-picking on small plots.

## Blackfly (Aphis fabae) on broad beans

Pinch out the growing tip once first pods set; that's the bit they want.

## Cabbage root fly

Collars of carpet underlay or DPM around the stem at planting.

## Clubroot

Soil-borne brassica disease. Once present, hard to eliminate. Liming the soil to pH 7+ helps; raise seedlings in modules so they have a strong root system before transplant.

## Vine weevil

Devastating on container-grown plants (heuchera, primula, fuchsia, strawberry). Nematodes (*Steinernema kraussei*) applied in autumn and spring.

## Codling moth on apples and pears

Pheromone traps for monitoring (and modest control); birds and parasitic wasps for biological control.

## Gooseberry sawfly

Strips bushes overnight in early summer. Hand-pick the larvae; encourage birds.

## Powdery mildew

Common on courgette, squash, cucumber, peas, roses, especially in dry weather following wet. Mulch heavily, water at the base, don't water foliage. Resistant cultivars exist.

## Apple scab, pear scab, peach leaf curl, black spot on roses

Fungal; cultivar resistance is the main approach in an organic system; covering peaches and nectarines from January to May prevents leaf curl.

## How to write threat content for Grow Daisy

- Lead with the plant species or symptom the user is dealing with.
- Name the pest or disease precisely (common name + Latin name when there's room).
- Give the organic / cultural control first, the chemical control last (where any chemical applies).
- Note seasonality if it matters — "carrot fly is worst May–July".
- Don't moralise about chemicals; describe what they do and let the user choose.

## Out of scope

Anything more obscure than the rota above, reach for the RHS pest pages.
