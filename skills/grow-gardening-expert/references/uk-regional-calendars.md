# UK Regional Climate Frame and Calendars

**Read this when:** drafting any timing-sensitive content (sowing, planting out, harvest), writing per-region calendars, or thinking about where in the UK a given operation works.

## The UK climate frame

UK gardeners do not think in USDA zones. They think in regions, postcode areas, microclimate, aspect, and altitude. A skill that gives American-zone advice on a UK page reads as foreign at best, condescending at worst.

**South-west and south coast (Cornwall, Devon, Dorset, Hampshire, parts of Sussex)** — frost-free much of the year in maritime spots; last frost typically late March to mid-April; first frost November or sometimes not at all in seaside gardens. RHS H2 plants often survive outdoors here. Growing season can start late February.

**Southern England and South Wales** — last frost mid-to-late April; first frost late October to mid-November. The default mental frame for most "UK gardening" advice because it's where the population centres are.

**Midlands, East Anglia, North-west England, Welsh borders** — last frost late April to early May; first frost early to mid-November. East Anglia is dry and continental — heat-loving crops do well, but frost can be sharp. The north-west is wetter; slugs are the dominant problem.

**North-east England and southern / central Scotland (Lowlands)** — last frost early to mid-May; first frost mid-October to early November. Growing season is shorter; choose early-maturing varieties.

**Highlands, north Wales uplands, Pennines, Lake District (upland everywhere)** — last frost late May, occasionally into June; first frost late September. Add a fortnight to any "national" sowing date. Plants rated RHS H4 may struggle.

**Ireland (all of it)** — maritime, mild, wet. Frost-free most years on the west coast; central and northern uplands see frosts later than the equivalent latitude in Britain because of the Atlantic moderation. The growing season is long; slugs and blight are the constant concerns.

**Microclimate and aspect always trump regional averages.** A south-facing walled garden in Yorkshire can grow figs and peaches. A north-facing garden in Surrey will struggle with tomatoes outdoors. When advising, name the assumption ("assuming an average garden in southern England with reasonable shelter…") rather than pretending one date works for everyone.

## Calendar shift template

UK sowing dates shift by roughly 1–2 weeks per region. When stating a date, qualify it. **Don't give a single national number for a frost-sensitive operation.**

A rough template for any "when to sow / plant out / harvest" question:

- **South-west / south coast:** [earliest date]
- **Southern England / South Wales:** [+1 to 2 weeks]
- **Midlands / East Anglia / NW England:** [+2 to 3 weeks]
- **NE England / Lowland Scotland:** [+3 to 4 weeks]
- **Highlands / uplands:** [+4 to 6 weeks; some operations not possible outdoors]

## Worked example — tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)

| Operation | South-west | Southern England | Midlands | NE/Scotland | Highlands |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sow seed under glass | mid-Feb | late-Feb to early-Mar | mid-Mar | late Mar | early Apr |
| Pot on | early Apr | mid-Apr | late Apr | early May | mid-May |
| Plant out (greenhouse) | mid-Apr | early May | mid-May | late May | early Jun |
| Plant out (outdoor) | mid-May | late May | early Jun | mid-Jun | usually under cover |
| First fruit | mid-Jul | late Jul | early Aug | mid-Aug | late Aug |
| Last fruit | mid-Oct | late Sep to early Oct | late Sep | early Sep | late Aug |

## Worked example — broad bean (autumn-sown)

| Operation | Most of UK |
|---|---|
| Sow direct (autumn) | late Oct to mid-Nov |
| Pinch tops (blackfly defence) | when first pods set, May–Jun |
| Harvest | late May to early Jul |

## Worked example — garlic

| Operation | Most of UK |
|---|---|
| Plant cloves (autumn) | mid-Oct to mid-Nov (hardneck); slightly later for softneck |
| Spring planting alternative | Feb–early Mar (softneck only; lower yield) |
| Harvest | mid-Jun to late Jul, when half the foliage has yellowed |

## Canonical structure for "when to grow X" content

For Grow Daisy quick-answer leads, FAQ answers, and species page calendars, use this structure:

1. Quick national answer with a typical mid-UK date and a number in the first sentence
2. Region table or bullet list with the south → north shift
3. Caveats — sheltered gardens earlier; upland gardens later; outdoor vs under-cover difference
4. Adjacent operations (pricking out, hardening off, succession sowing) where relevant
