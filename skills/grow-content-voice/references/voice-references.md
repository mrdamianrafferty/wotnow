# Voice References — Who to Read

**Read this when:** drafting Grow Daisy long-form content and you want to ground yourself in the right register before writing. Also when assessing whether a draft is in the right voice.

## Primary references — read these to absorb the voice

### Monty Don

- *The Complete Gardener* (2003, revised 2021)
- *Around the World in 80 Gardens* (2008)
- Long-form pieces for *Gardeners' World* magazine
- His monthly diary

**What to absorb:** warmth, willingness to notice weather and season, the way a sentence can be long without being baggy, scientific grounding worn lightly, occasional sentimentality that earns its place.

**Watch out for:** Don occasionally tips into the smug register — particularly the "long view" mode where the writer positions themselves as someone with perspective. Borrow the noticing, not the positioning.

### Beth Chatto

- *Beth Chatto's Damp Garden* (1982)
- *Beth Chatto's Gravel Garden* (2000)
- *Beth Chatto's Green Tapestry* (1989)

**What to absorb:** ecological grounding, the discipline of "right plant, right place", plantswoman-precise observation, sentence economy. Chatto wastes no words.

**Watch out for:** very little — Chatto is one of the cleanest voices in the canon. Occasional gardener-specific assumptions about reader knowledge; check whether the Grow Daisy reader needs more setup.

### Charles Dowding

- *No Dig Gardening, Course 1 & 2*
- His blog and YouTube — [charlesdowding.co.uk](https://charlesdowding.co.uk/)
- His diary posts and harvest records

**What to absorb:** the practical voice in its purest form. Imperative, direct, observational. Reads like the diary of a working market gardener — because it is. Every sentence does work; nothing decorative.

**Watch out for:** Dowding has strong opinions about no-dig — fine to disagree, but disagree with the evidence not the man.

### Margery Fish

- *We Made a Garden* (1956)

**What to absorb:** affection without sentimentality. The "and then we did this" cadence of someone telling you how they made something. Useful for blog posts about the Grow Daisy team's own gardens.

## Secondary references — read for contrast

### Christopher Lloyd

Brilliant, but the smug tendency is there. Read for the eye, the willingness to be opinionated, the deep plant knowledge. Don't borrow the attitude.

### Vita Sackville-West

Historical interest mainly. Voice is class-locked in a way that doesn't transfer to a modern UK app audience. Read to understand what we're not.

### Most contemporary commercial gardening blogs

Corporate, hedged, SEO-stuffed. "X is one of the most popular… It is recommended that… Many gardeners prefer…" Read to know the failure mode of consumer gardening content on the web.

## How to use references when drafting

1. **Before drafting a long-form piece**, read 2–3 pages of whichever voice you're aiming for. Don't paraphrase — absorb cadence.
2. **Draft without the reference open** — you want their pulse, not their words.
3. **Read your draft aloud.** Smug writing reads worse aloud than on the page. Editorial writing reads better aloud than on the page.
4. **Cut 20%.** First drafts in any voice are 20% too long.
