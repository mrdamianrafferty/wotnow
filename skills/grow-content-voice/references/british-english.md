# British English Conventions

**Read this when:** writing any Grow Daisy English source content. The DeepL pipeline translates from this source into other languages — if it's not British English here, the whole multilingual stack drifts.

## Vocabulary

| Use | Don't use |
|---|---|
| courgette | zucchini |
| aubergine | eggplant |
| sweet pepper | bell pepper |
| coriander (leaf) | cilantro |
| swede | rutabaga |
| garden | yard (except "yard" as a unit) |
| compost heap, compost bin | compost pile |
| flowerbed, vegetable bed, raised bed | garden patch |
| allotment, veg patch, kitchen garden, potager | vegetable garden |
| glasshouse / greenhouse / polytunnel / cold frame | "indoors" as a catch-all |
| fleece (frost protection) | row cover (US) |
| autumn | fall |
| harvest, lift, pick, cut, pull | harvest only |
| mulch with… | top-dress with… (these are different operations) |
| pricking out | transplanting (in the seedling sense) |

## Spelling

- -ise not -ize (organise, fertiliser, naturalise)
- -our not -or (colour, flavour, vigour, behaviour)
- -re not -er (centre, fibre, metre — except in the units where it stays metre)
- aluminium not aluminum
- mould not mold (the soil amendment and the fungus alike)
- grey not gray
- Defence not defense (when discussing pest defence)

## When to use Latin names

Use the Latin binomial where it:

- **Disambiguates** between species commonly given the same English name ("rocket" the salad vs. "rocket" the firework — silly example; but "marjoram" can mean *Origanum majorana* or *O. vulgare* and you should say which)
- **Names a specific cultivar or hybrid** being recommended (*Lavandula × intermedia* 'Hidcote Giant')
- **Names the genus** when discussing range and variation across it (Salvia, Allium, Brassica)
- The reader is at the point in the article where they'd benefit from looking the plant up

Don't use the Latin name where the English is unambiguous and the reader is in the opening paragraph trying to get their question answered.

## Numbers, dates, and units

- **Dates:** "mid-March", "early April", "the second week of May", "late October" — describe by week-of-month or fortnight rather than exact dates that won't hold from year to year.
- **Lengths:** centimetres for plant spacing, metres for plot dimensions, occasional inches and feet where the gardener thinks in them (hedge height, fence height).
- **Temperatures:** °C only.
- **Time durations:** "10 days", "a fortnight", "6 weeks", "the first season".
- **Quantities:** "a handful of compost", "a bucket of leaf mould" are fine — gardeners measure by the handful and the bucket.

## House style for headers and labels

- **H1 on a plant page:** "Tomatoes (*Solanum lycopersicum*) — UK growing guide". Common name (plural if appropriate), Latin name in italics in parentheses, em-dash, qualifier.
- **H2 patterns:** "When to sow tomatoes in the UK", "Where to grow tomatoes", "How to plant out", "Common problems", "Companion plants". Match the question form for AI extraction.
- **Sentence case** for headings, not Title Case. ("How to grow tomatoes", not "How To Grow Tomatoes".)
- **Em-dashes** for asides, not hyphens. UK gardening writing uses em-dashes generously.

## Punctuation

- Single quote marks for emphasis ('Resistafly' cultivar); double quotes only for actual speech.
- Oxford comma not required, but used where it disambiguates.
- Numerals from 10 upwards; "one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine" spelt out unless in a measurement.
