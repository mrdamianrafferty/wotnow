# Voice Mode — Editorial / Contemplative

**Read this when:** writing species pages (especially the About paragraph and any long-form section), blog posts, hub pages, monthly highlights, the "in your garden this week" rail, or any place where the gardener is reading at leisure rather than mid-task.

## Touchstones

- **Monty Don** for warmth and the willingness to notice weather and season
- **Beth Chatto** for ecological grounding and the discipline of "right plant, right place"
- A dash of **Margery Fish** for plantswoman's affection without sentimentality

## Cadence

Mixed sentence lengths. Some long ones that earn their length by going somewhere. Some short ones that land. Paragraphs of 2–4 sentences. Latin names where they disambiguate (*Allium cepa* the onion, not "onion the genus Allium"), not where they decorate.

## What this voice notices

- Time of year, weather conditions
- The smell of the leaves when crushed, the precise tone of a yellow autumn rather than just "autumn"
- The gardener's relationship to a plant — what it costs to grow, what it gives back
- Place: where in the UK, what soil, what aspect
- Memory and continuity — that gardens are made over years

## What this voice avoids

- Encyclopaedia-tone "Lactuca sativa is an annual leaf vegetable of the family Asteraceae" openings
- "Of course", "naturally", "as any gardener knows", "needless to say"
- The word "harness", "leverage", "unlock", "transform", "elevate"
- Hyperbole — no "stunning", "spectacular", "amazing" unless something genuinely is
- Manifesto-tone — no "we believe that…"
- Lists where prose would do
- Footnotes of qualifications appended to every claim

## Example — opening for a species page (rhubarb)

> Rhubarb is one of the few crops worth growing for the months you're not eating it. The crowns sit underground all winter, knotted and unpromising, and then in February — earlier under a forced pot — push up tight crimson knuckles that uncurl into the first proper green of the year. By April you have stalks the colour of a flag. By June the plant is a metre across and giving you more than you can use. Crowns last twenty years if you look after them. They want sun, deep moist soil, and an annual mulch of whatever rotted compost you can spare.

That's editorial voice. It opens with an observation the reader will recognise, gets to the practical point inside three sentences, and includes the timing (Feb to June) and the conditions (sun, deep moist soil, annual mulch) without lecturing. No Latin in the opening — *Rheum × hybridum* belongs lower down in the Quick Facts card, not here.

## Counter-example — what this voice is not

> Rhubarb (Rheum × hybridum) is a perennial vegetable native to Asia. It is widely cultivated in the United Kingdom for its long, tart, edible stalks. The leaves contain oxalic acid and should not be consumed. Rhubarb prefers well-drained, fertile soil in a sunny position. It is a long-lived crop, persisting for many years once established.

Wikipedia voice. Accurate, useless. Nobody enjoys reading it.

## Counter-example — the smug version

> The dedicated grower will, of course, want to bring their rhubarb crowns into production as early as possible. Forcing — a technique practised for generations on the great Yorkshire estates — produces those tender pink stalks that are the mark of a proper kitchen garden.

This is what we are not. Class-marker language, "the dedicated grower", invoking "the great Yorkshire estates" to lend the prose borrowed authority. See `anti-smugness.md` for the full check.

## When editorial isn't right

If the reader is mid-task (in-app task description, push notification, error state), use **practical voice** (`voice-practical.md`). If the page needs an AI-quotable opening paragraph (every species page does), use **quick-answer voice** (`voice-quick-answer.md`) for the first 80–150 words and editorial voice for the rest.
